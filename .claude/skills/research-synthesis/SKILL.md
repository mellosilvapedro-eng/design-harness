# research-synthesis

The failure mode of design research is a mood board with a number attached. This skill
is about producing something you can decide from.

## Reading a reference

For each reference, answer:

- **What problem is it solving?** Often not the one it appears to solve.
- **What does it trade away?** A reference with no visible cost has an invisible one.
- **What is transferable, and what is theirs?** Separate mechanism from decoration.
- **Would it survive our constraints?** Data volume, design system, permissions, audience.

Five read this way beat thirty collected. A reference that fails these is inspiration,
not evidence; label it or drop it.

## Pattern vs anomaly

| | Means | Do |
|---|---|---|
| **pattern** | 3+ products converged, plausibly independently | strong prior; deviating needs a stated reason |
| **convention** | everyone does it, including those who should not | check whether it is good or inherited |
| **anomaly** | one product does it differently | insight or mistake; work out which before borrowing |

Convergence is weak when everyone copied the same original. Three copies are one data point.

## What data can and cannot prove

Analytics shows **where** and **how many**, never **why**.

- **A drop-off is not a problem** until you know what the step is for.
- **Volume is not importance.** Rare flows are often the high-stakes ones.
- **Missing events are not absent behaviour.** Check it was instrumented.
- **Averages hide the shape.** Two groups averaged describe neither.
- **Survivorship.** The data holds only those who got far enough to be measured.

**Discover the event taxonomy before querying.** A guessed event name returns a
confident wrong number. When there is no quantitative signal, say so and switch to
heuristic and comparative evaluation.

## Writing the insight

Not a fact, not a recommendation: a fact plus a tension that implies action.

- fact: "62% abandon the request at the approver step"
- insight: "They abandon there because they do not know who approves until that screen"
- recommendation: "Show the approver chain at the start"

Format: **what we saw**, **what we think it means**, **how confident and what would
change our mind**. An insight nobody can falsify is an opinion.

## Holding the contradiction

When references and data disagree, **that is the finding**. Write both and name the
tension. Do not average them into something that decides nothing.

## The output

Into `research.md` or `directions.md`:

1. **What we now know**: 3 to 6 insights.
2. **What we still do not know**, and whether it is worth finding out.
3. **References**: problem, trade-off, what is transferable.
4. **Limitations**: what ran degraded, what was unavailable, what is inferred. Not optional.
